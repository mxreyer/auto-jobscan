## One-line

Mechanical engineer and field biologist who designed, built, and then lived
inside life-critical hardware: twelve years running a one-person pressurized
research station, owning everything from the pressure vessel and its life
support to the instrumentation and the science it existed to produce.

## Status

US citizen, no security clearance. Relocating to Texas — Austin, Houston,
Dallas or San Antonio — after twelve years at a remote field station. Remote
is acceptable, though she would rather be co-located with a team for a while.
Travel unlimited.

Targeting **both** industry hardware engineering and research-science roles,
and they are weighted equally. Role type is NOT a filter, capability fit is.

## Demonstrated capability

**Life-critical pressure & life-support systems (deepest strength)**
- Designed, built and operated a one-atmosphere pressurized habitat rated for
  continuous human occupancy at depth, and lived in it for twelve years with
  zero loss-of-pressure events.
- Owned the full life-support stack: oxygen generation, CO2 scrubbing,
  humidity control, leak detection and alarms. Rebuilt the scrubber loop twice
  after finding degradation patterns she had not anticipated.
- Seal, penetrator and weld specification for continuous saltwater immersion;
  devised the corrosion programme herself because none existed.
- Leak checking as reflex rather than procedure — the hardware had no
  possibility of a maintenance visit.

**Prototype vehicles & manipulators**
- Built and flew a sounding rocket from scratch — airframe, propulsion
  integration, recovery, guidance. Two uncrewed test flights failed and were
  documented; the third flew crewed, only after the vehicle had survived test.
- Designed and operated submersibles and remote manipulators for sample
  collection, including a dexterous end-effector that handles soft-bodied
  invertebrates without damaging them. Compliance, force sensing and knowing
  when to stop were the requirement, not features added later.
- Built a powered exoskeleton for surface excursion: joint torque density,
  thermal management inside an enclosed suit, and the human-factors problem of
  a suit nobody will wear if it is uncomfortable. It was unwearable for its
  first two years.

**Field science & instrumentation**
- Twelve years of continuous marine ecology and behavioural observation at a
  single site, producing a longitudinal dataset on behaviour, population
  dynamics and interspecies interaction.
- Continuous residence rather than episodic sampling is the methodological
  contribution — it captures behaviours expedition-based work systematically
  misses.
- Built her own instrumentation where nothing suitable existed: automated
  water-chemistry loggers, non-destructive specimen handling.
- 3 peer-reviewed publications; ~15 conference presentations.

**Solo end-to-end delivery (single-investigator scale — see caveat)**
- Every project above was specified, funded, designed, fabricated, tested and
  operated by one person, somewhere a missing part meant designing around it
  rather than reordering.
- Writes the test plan before the design, adopted deliberately after the rocket
  failures, specifically so that verification cannot be negotiated away later.
- Supervised field volunteers and visiting students on sampling and observation
  protocols. Numbers not recorded.
- Caveat: this is depth without organisational context — no team, no review
  process, no one checking the work.

## Languages & tools

Mechanical design and hand calculation, CAD (self-taught), machining, welding
and fabrication, electronics prototyping, sensor integration, systems
integration, test and validation, Python, MATLAB, R, field methods and
experimental design, technical writing, scientific diving, small-craft
operation.

## Honest gaps — do NOT score as if present

- **No industry engineering role.** Twelve years at a single-investigator field
  station: no company, no product, no customer, no schedule set by anyone else.
- **Nothing built to an external standard.** No AS9100, NASA-STD, ASME BPVC or
  equivalent. The habitat is safe because she is careful, not because anyone
  signed off on it. Note that one archived cover letter claims "extensive
  experience qualifying flight hardware to demanding standards" — that claim is
  not supported by anything else in the record and must not be scored.
- **Every build is a batch of one.** No design for manufacture, no tolerance
  stack-ups for volume, no repeat production, no released-drawing practice.
- **Modern toolchain is thin.** CAD is self-taught; FEA and CFD have never been
  used in anger; no simulation tooling and no *institutional* verification
  process — test plans, procedures and records are her own practice, never
  anyone's standard.
- **No team engineering, and no organisational context at all.** No direct
  reports, no design review by anyone empowered to reject the design, never
  worked inside someone else's requirements document, no stakeholders, no
  review cycle — for twelve years.
- **No people management and no budget ownership.** Supervising volunteers on
  field protocols is neither.
- **No supplier or vendor management.** Never qualified a supplier, never owned
  a procurement or a build-to-print relationship.
- **No security clearance**, which gates a large share of Texas aerospace work.
