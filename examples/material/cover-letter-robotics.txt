Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing about the Staff Mechanical Engineer opening on your dexterity
team.

I have spent twelve years building manipulators that had to pick up living
animals without hurting them, underwater, at pressure, with no chance to
iterate quickly if I got it wrong. The end-effector I am proudest of handles
a soft-bodied invertebrate without damaging it, which turned out to be a
harder problem than any rigid-object grasping I have done. Compliance,
force sensing, and knowing when to stop are not features I added later --
they were the requirement.

I have also built a powered exoskeleton for surface excursion. It taught me
more about actuation than the rest of my career combined, mostly through
being unwearable for the first two years. Joint torque density, thermal
management in an enclosed suit, and the fact that a human being will simply
refuse to use something uncomfortable regardless of how well it performs --
I learned all of that the slow way.

I should be straightforward about what I have not done. I have never worked
in industry. I have never built the same thing twice, never designed for
manufacture, and never had a design reviewed by someone whose job was to
find the flaw in it. Everything I have made is a batch of one, built by the
person who specified it, which is a way of working I now think has real
limits.

What I would bring is twelve years of hardware that could not be allowed to
fail, and a very high tolerance for the unglamorous part of the work.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sandy Cheeks
