# Saved answers to recurring application questions

Reused across applications. Kept so I stop rewriting the same paragraph.

---

**Are you legally authorized to work in the United States?**

Yes — US citizen.

**Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?**

No.

**Do you currently hold, or have you previously held, a US security clearance?**

No. I understand this rules me out of a good portion of the work in Texas and
I am willing to go through the process.

**Are you willing to relocate?**

I am relocating regardless. Texas — Austin, Houston, Dallas or San Antonio.
Remote is fine too, though I would rather be in a building with other people
for a while.

**Willing to travel?**

Yes, without limit. I have been unreachable for twelve years; a business trip
does not frighten me.

---

**Why are you leaving your current role?**

I built the station to answer a question, and I have answered it. What I have
now is twelve years of doing every job myself, which has made me capable and
has also stopped teaching me anything. I want to be the least experienced
person on a good team for a while.

**Describe a time you failed.**

The rocket. First attempt, the recovery system deployed under thrust and took
the airframe apart about two seconds after ignition. I had bench-tested the
deployment charge and I had bench-tested the flight computer, but I had never
tested them together under vibration, because building the shake table felt
like a detour from the actual project.

It was not a detour. The second attempt failed differently, for a related
reason. The third flew.

What I took from it is that I am good at the parts of engineering I find
interesting and I will quietly skip the parts I do not, and the parts I skip
are disproportionately the verification. I now write the test plan before the
design, specifically so that I cannot negotiate with myself about it later.

**Describe a time you worked on a team.**

I want to answer this one honestly rather than well. I have not, not in the
sense the question means. I have supervised volunteers on field protocols,
and I have collaborated with co-authors at a distance. I have not been on an
engineering team, I have never had a design reviewed by someone empowered to
reject it, and I have never had to work inside somebody else's requirements
document.

I think that is the biggest thing missing from my résumé and the main reason
someone might reasonably pass on me.

**What is your greatest strength?**

I have designed things I then had to trust with my own life, continuously, for
over a decade. It gives you a particular relationship with margin.

**Salary expectations?**

Genuinely unsure — I have been outside the market for twelve years and I do
not have a calibrated number. I would rather hear the range for the role.

**Anything else we should know?**

My background is unusual and I would rather flag the gaps than have them
discovered. No industry experience. Nothing I have built was certified to an
external standard — the habitat is safe because I am careful, not because
anyone signed off on it. Every build is a batch of one. My CAD is self-taught
and I have never used FEA in anger.

I am aware of how that reads next to the résumé. Both are accurate; the résumé
is just the half that gets you an interview.
